Common Problems

Your Symptom Tells Us the Part

Ice makers are the one component where the exact symptom is genuinely diagnostic. No ice, poor ice and odd ice are three different faults, and knowing which you have saves a return visit.

🚫 No Ice At All

First question is whether water is reaching the unit. A closed supply valve, a kinked line behind the cabinet or a filter long overdue for changing will stop production completely, and none of those is a broken ice maker. If water is arriving and nothing happens, we are looking at the inlet valve or the module itself.

🫗 Hollow or Undersized Cubes

Water is getting in, just not enough of it. That is usually the inlet valve not opening fully, a filter that has clogged, or low supply pressure to the house line. It is worth acting on, because the same restriction that makes thin cubes often ends in a frozen fill tube.

🧊 Fill Tube Frozen Shut

A little water lingers in the tube after each fill and eventually freezes into a plug. Then nothing gets through at all. Thawing it is the easy half; the repair that lasts is fixing whatever let the water sit there, usually a valve that is not closing cleanly or a cabinet running colder than it should.

🧱 Cubes Fusing Into One Block

Common in households that use little ice. Cubes sit, surface-melt slightly, refreeze into each other, and the ejector eventually jams against the mass. Sometimes the cause is just low usage, but a cabinet cycling through temperature swings will do it too, and that is worth finding.

👃 Ice Tastes or Smells Wrong

Almost never the ice maker. Old ice absorbs odours from whatever is around it, and a filter past its service life passes everything through. Dump the bin, change the filter, and give it two full cycles. If it still tastes wrong after that, the problem is in the water line and we should look.

💧 Water Under or Inside the Unit

A fill valve that does not close fully keeps weeping after the cycle ends. The water freezes somewhere it should not, or finds its way out below. On a built-in this is worth prompt attention, because the water ends up against cabinetry rather than on an open floor.

Models We Service

JennAir Ice Makers We Work On

Tell us whether the ice maker sits in the freezer, in the door, or in a dedicated drawer. It changes which assembly we bring.

Freezer Compartment Ice Makers

The standard assembly mounted inside the freezer on built-in and column units, with its own fill tube and valve.

In-Door Ice Makers

Mounted in the refrigerator door on side-by-side units. Tighter access and a longer fill path, so frozen tubes come up more often here.

French Door Drawer Units

Ice maker in the upper compartment with the bin below. Bin level sensors and ejector jams are common on these.

Column Refrigerator Ice Makers

Fitted in refrigerator columns. Independent of the freezer column entirely, which surprises people when one works and the other does not.

Undercounter Ice Machines

Dedicated ice units under the counter. Drain and water supply faults dominate rather than module failures.
