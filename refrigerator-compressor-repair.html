Short Answer

Read This Before You Agree to a Compressor

Compressors are blamed far more often than they actually fail. A start relay, a run capacitor, an overload protector, a condenser fan that has stopped, or a control board that never sends the start signal all produce the same symptoms as a dead compressor — a hum, a click, a warm cabinet — and all of them cost a fraction of a compressor job. Those get measured before the word compressor is used.

If someone has quoted you a compressor replacement without putting gauges on the sealed system and measuring the start components, you do not have a diagnosis. You have a guess, and it is a guess that happens to be the most expensive answer available.

This matters most on built-in refrigeration. A compressor job on a Sub-Zero, Thermador, Viking or Wolf column is real work, but it is a fraction of replacing a unit that was fitted into cabinetry — so the answer there is almost always repair. The place to be careful is a mass-market freestanding refrigerator, where a genuine compressor failure can genuinely be the end of the appliance.

Quick Answer

What a Failed Compressor Actually Means

A failed refrigerator compressor usually shows up as clicking or humming followed by shutoff, a warm fridge with a hot compressor housing, a unit that runs constantly without reaching temperature, or a tripped breaker on restart. A start relay or capacitor — the parts most often mistaken for a dead compressor — sits at the cheap end of this category. A full compressor replacement on a standard refrigerator is a different order of job, and sealed-system compressor work on a built-in is different again. Which one you are actually looking at is what the diagnostic settles. The $90 diagnostic fee is waived if you approve the repair.

Repair vs. replace comes down to the unit. On built-in brands — Sub-Zero, Thermador, Viking, Wolf — a compressor repair is almost always worth it: replacing a built-in refrigerator means the appliance plus the cabinetry it was fitted into, so even a sealed-system repair is a fraction of that. On mass-market refrigerators 8+ years old, the math often flips — once a compressor job is added to an aging unit's remaining lifespan, a new refrigerator can be the better spend. We tell you which situation you're in during the diagnostic, before you decide anything.

Warning Signs

Symptoms of a Failing Compressor

Clicking or Humming, Then Shutoff

The compressor tries to start, can't, and cuts out on its overload protector — often a bad start relay or capacitor, sometimes the compressor itself.

Warm Fridge, Hot Compressor

The compressor housing (rear, bottom) is hot to the touch but the cabinet isn't cooling — a classic sign the compressor is running but not pumping refrigerant.

Runs Constantly

The unit never cycles off and still can't hold temperature — the compressor is working overtime and losing.

Tripped Breaker on Restart

A seized or shorted compressor can pull enough current to trip a breaker every time it tries to start.

Sealed-System Work Most Shops Decline

Compressor replacement means opening, evacuating, and recharging the sealed refrigerant system — work that legally requires EPA Section 608 refrigerant handling certification. Most handymen and general repair services turn this job away. We don't.
