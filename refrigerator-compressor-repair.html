Quick Answer

What a Failed Compressor Means and What It Costs

A failed refrigerator compressor usually shows up as clicking or humming followed by shutoff, a warm fridge with a hot compressor housing, a unit that runs constantly without reaching temperature, or a tripped breaker on restart. Repair costs range from $185-$350 for a start relay or capacitor (often mistaken for a dead compressor) up to $800-$1,500 for a full compressor replacement on a standard refrigerator, and $1,200-$2,200 for sealed-system compressor work on built-in brands. The $90 diagnostic fee is waived if you approve the repair.

Repair vs. replace comes down to the unit. On built-in brands — Sub-Zero, Thermador, Viking, Wolf — a compressor repair is almost always worth it: replacing a built-in refrigerator runs $10,000 or more, so even a $2,000 sealed-system repair is a fraction of that cost. On mass-market refrigerators 8+ years old, the math often flips — once a compressor job is added to an aging unit's remaining lifespan, a new refrigerator can be the better spend. We tell you which situation you're in during the diagnostic, before you decide anything.

Warning Signs

Symptoms of a Failing Compressor

Clicking or Humming, Then Shutoff

The compressor tries to start, can't, and cuts out on its overload protector — often a bad start relay or capacitor, sometimes the compressor itself.

Warm Fridge, Hot Compressor

The compressor housing (rear, bottom) is hot to the touch but the cabinet isn't cooling — a classic sign the compressor is running but not pumping refrigerant.

Runs Constantly

The unit never cycles off and still can't hold temperature — the compressor is working overtime and losing.

Tripped Breaker on Restart

A seized or shorted compressor can pull enough current to trip a breaker every time it tries to start.

Sealed-System Work Most Shops Decline

Compressor replacement means opening, evacuating, and recharging the sealed refrigerant system — work that legally requires EPA Section 608 refrigerant handling certification. Most handymen and general repair services turn this job away. We don't.
