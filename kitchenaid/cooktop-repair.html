Short Answer

What Usually Goes Wrong with a KitchenAid Cooktop

On gas models: spark electrodes wet or contaminated after a boil-over, a cracked switch in the spark circuit, or burner ports blocked with cooked-on spill. On radiant glass: a failed surface element, an infinite switch that has stopped cycling, or cracked glass. On induction: a dead coil or its driver board, and the “pan not detected” complaint that is usually the cookware rather than the appliance.

Two of those — the gas ignition faults and the dead radiant element — account for the large majority of the calls, and both are straightforward parts repairs on a KitchenAid. The one that decides whether you repair or replace at all is the glass, and it is worth understanding before you spend anything.

Work out which machine you have first. If the surface is a smooth black or white ceramic sheet and the zones glow red-orange through it, that is radiant. If the surface is identical but nothing glows and the pan itself gets hot while the surface stays relatively cool, that is induction. If there are grates and burners, that is gas. People routinely call an induction cooktop “electric,” and the two have almost nothing in common underneath.

Do This First

Four Checks Before You Pay for a Service Call

  1. If it is clicking, dry it out. Lift the grates and caps, and leave the burner area completely dry for a few hours — overnight is better. A boil-over that reaches the electrode makes the spark module fire continuously because the moisture gives it a path to ground. A large share of “my cooktop won’t stop clicking” calls resolve themselves at this step at no cost.
  2. Reseat the burner cap. Lift it, check the underside and the burner head are clean, and set it back down flat so it sits square in its recess. A cap sitting a millimetre proud will give you a burner that sparks but never lights, or one that lights with a lopsided uneven flame.
  3. Put a magnet on the pan. Induction only. If a fridge magnet does not stick firmly to the base of the pan, the cooktop is working correctly and the cookware is the problem. Aluminium, copper and most non-magnetic stainless will not work, and neither will a pan much smaller than the zone.
  4. Check both breaker legs. Electric and induction cooktops are fed by a double-pole breaker. If one leg opens, you can get a cooktop that is entirely dead, or one where some zones work and others do not, while the breaker handle still looks perfectly normal. Switch it fully off and back on before assuming the control board has failed.

The Decision

On a Glass Cooktop, the Glass Decides It

On radiant and induction models the ceramic glass top is typically the single most expensive component in the appliance. When the glass is cracked, that one part plus the labour to fit it frequently lands close enough to the price of a new cooktop that replacing makes better sense — and we will tell you so rather than sell you the repair.

A crack is also not cosmetic. The glass is the barrier between you and live elements or induction coils, and a crack lets liquid through to the wiring beneath. A cooktop with a crack running through a zone should be switched off at the breaker and left off, whatever you decide to do next.

The flip side is worth saying just as plainly: everything that is not the glass is usually worth repairing. Elements, infinite switches, spark modules, electrodes, touch interfaces and control boards are all ordinary parts on a KitchenAid, and the repair is a fraction of replacement. The temptation with an appliance this age is to write it off wholesale after one component fails; that is rarely the right call here.

Why KitchenAid Is Different

Parts Availability Changes the Maths

KitchenAid sits in an unusual spot: built and specified like a premium appliance, but part of a large manufacturing family, which means the components behind it are ordinary trade stock rather than proprietary rarities. That matters more than it sounds.

Common parts are genuinely available

Surface elements, infinite switches, spark modules, electrodes and igniters are stocked items. On a luxury European brand the same fault can mean a special order and a wait; here it often does not. That is the main reason a KitchenAid repair is usually worth doing.

The constraint is boards, not burners

Where an older KitchenAid runs out of road is electronics. Control boards and touch interfaces on discontinued models do get superseded and eventually vanish. We check availability before you spend on a diagnostic conclusion you cannot act on.

Built-in means the opening decides too

A cooktop is cut into a countertop. If yours is a size that is no longer standard, replacing it can mean stone work as well as an appliance. On those, repair keeps winning well past the point you would expect.

FAQ

KitchenAid Cooktop Repair Questions

Why does my KitchenAid gas cooktop keep clicking?

Nearly always moisture. A boil-over reaches the spark electrode, the moisture gives the spark a path to ground, and the module fires continuously — often on every burner at once. Lift the grates and caps and let the area dry thoroughly, ideally overnight. If it is still clicking dry, it is a switch stuck closed or a failing spark module, and that is a parts repair.

One burner sparks but never lights. What is wrong?

Start with the burner cap. If it is not sitting flat and square in its recess, the burner will spark and never catch. After that: ports blocked with cooked-on spill, which clears with cleaning, or a cracked electrode that is sparking to the wrong place. All three are inexpensive outcomes.

My cooktop element is stuck on high and the knob does nothing. Is that dangerous?

Yes, treat it as urgent. That is an infinite switch with welded contacts, which means the zone is receiving power regardless of the knob position and cannot be switched off at the control. Turn the cooktop off at the breaker and leave it off until it is repaired. The switch itself is a routine part.

Is a cracked glass cooktop worth repairing?

Often not, and we will say so. The ceramic glass is usually the most expensive single part in the appliance, so replacing it plus labour frequently approaches the cost of a new cooktop. It is also a safety issue rather than a cosmetic one, because the glass is what separates you from live elements. Switch it off at the breaker and get an honest assessment before spending anything.

My induction cooktop says the pan is not detected. Is the cooktop broken?

Usually not. Induction only works with ferrous cookware — put a fridge magnet on the base of the pan, and if it does not stick firmly the cooktop is behaving correctly. Pans significantly smaller than the zone also fail to register. If magnetic, correctly sized cookware is not detected on one zone while other zones work, that points at the coil or its driver board.

The whole cooktop is dead but the breaker looks fine. What now?

Electric and induction cooktops are fed by a double-pole breaker carrying two legs of power. If one leg opens, the appliance can appear completely dead — or behave strangely, with some functions alive and others not — while the breaker handle still sits in the on position. Switch it fully off and back on. If that does not restore it, the fault is in the supply, the junction box connection, or the control board.

Do you repair KitchenAid gas, radiant and induction cooktops?

All three, across Dallas-Fort Worth, along with downdraft models. They are genuinely different machines underneath, so telling us which you have when you call determines what goes on the truck and how long the visit takes.

Are KitchenAid cooktop parts hard to get?

The common ones are not. Elements, infinite switches, spark modules and electrodes are ordinary trade stock, which is a real advantage over some luxury brands where the same fault means a special order. The parts that do go obsolete are control boards and touch interfaces on discontinued models, so we check availability before recommending a repair you cannot actually complete.
