Common Problems

The KitchenAid Ice Faults We See Most

Before booking anything, notice which of these matches your situation. On ice makers the symptom narrows the part more reliably than on almost any other appliance.

🔇 You Never Hear It Cycle

A working ice maker is audible. It fills, it rests, it drops cubes with a distinct clatter. Silence for a full day usually means the module or the ejector motor has stopped rather than anything to do with water. If you can hear it filling but nothing ever drops, that is a different fault and a different part.

🚫 Bin Fills Once, Then Stops

Often the bin level arm or sensor rather than production. The unit thinks the bin is still full and stops making more, so you get one batch and then nothing. Easy to confuse with a dying ice maker, much cheaper to put right.

🫗 Cubes Small or Hollow

Water is arriving, just not enough. Usually the inlet valve not opening fully, a filter well past its service life, or low pressure on the house supply. Worth handling early, because the restriction that causes thin cubes is often what later freezes the fill tube solid.

🧊 Fill Tube Iced Over

Residual water sits in the tube after a fill and eventually builds into a plug. Thawing it takes minutes; making it stay fixed means finding why water was sitting there, normally a valve not closing cleanly or a compartment running colder than it should.

🧱 Ice Clumped Into One Mass

Cubes surface-melt and refreeze into each other, and the ejector jams against the result. Low household usage explains plenty of cases. When it keeps happening, the freezer temperature is swinging, and that is the thing to fix rather than the ice.

👃 Ice Tasting of the Freezer

Rarely the ice maker itself. Old ice absorbs whatever is stored around it, and a filter past its life passes flavours straight through. Empty the bin, change the filter, run two full cycles. If it still tastes wrong, the issue is upstream in the water line.
