Blackhawk Coil Cleaner: What We Use on Commercial Kitchen Refrigeration in DFW

Blackhawk is Nu-Calgon's fragrance-free alkaline coil cleaner built for kitchen application, listed for evaporator coils, condenser coils, permanent filters and air filters. We use it on True and Sub-Zero refrigeration condensers, ice machine condensers and walk-in coils where grease is the enemy and added fragrance is not welcome near food. Restaurant owners and anyone with a serious home kitchen should know why this is the jug we pull out.

What Blackhawk is

Blackhawk is a concentrated alkaline coil cleaner. The Nu-Calgon chart describes it in three phrases: kitchen application, robust and versatile, fragrance free. It is biodegradable, NSF Registered and microchannel safe, and its target soils are dirt and grease deposits.

The concentrate has a pH of 12.0 and the diluted product a pH of 10.0, the mildest of the heavy-duty alkaline concentrates on the chart. It is still caustic at full strength and is handled with gloves and eye protection, but its working dilutions are wide, and its most diluted mix is a no-rinse product.

What equipment we use it on (and what we don't)

Where the chart's four targets show up on our jobs in Dallas–Fort Worth:

  • Reach-in and under-counter refrigeration. True, Sub-Zero, U-Line and similar units in restaurants and premium home kitchens. The condenser sits low and pulls in grease aerosol and fryer vapor until the fins are glued together with a brown film.
  • Ice machine condensers. Hoshizaki and other air-cooled machines. A grease-fouled condenser is a common reason ice production drops in summer.
  • Walk-in cooler and freezer coils. The evaporator inside the box, where an added fragrance would sit in an enclosed space with open food, and the condensing unit outside. Cleaning only; we do not repair refrigeration systems on a coil cleaning visit.
  • Kitchen HVAC. Evaporator coils on rooftop and make-up air units serving dining rooms and kitchens.
  • Permanent and washable filters in kitchen air handlers.

What we do not use Blackhawk for: it is not a disinfectant, it is not listed for electronic air cleaner cells, and it is not our first choice where brightening an oxidized outdoor condenser is the goal. For a general-purpose truck product across residential and commercial we more often carry Tri-Pow'r HD.

Why this chemistry for this job

Kitchen soil is organic. Grease, oil vapor, food particles and the dust that binds to them respond to alkaline chemistry, which lifts fat off the fin. Acidic cleaners are for mineral scale and oxidation and are not what a greasy condenser needs. Blackhawk is alkaline, microchannel safe and listed as robust, meaning it handles heavy grease at its stronger dilutions.

Fragrance free is the feature that decides it in a kitchen. A scented cleaner on a condenser under a prep table or an evaporator in a walk-in puts that scent into the airflow around food. Blackhawk has no added fragrance, so the only thing you smell afterward is the absence of the old grease odor.

Blackhawk's dilution range is the widest of the heavy-duty cleaners on the chart: no rinse at 20:1, light soil at 15:1, medium soil at 10:1 and heavy soil at 5:1. The 20:1 no-rinse mix is a technician's choice for indoor coils where rinsing is impractical: an evaporator inside a walk-in stacked with product, or a reach-in condenser in a dining room during service. At 20:1 we apply, allow the 5–15 minute dwell the chart specifies, and let the condensate from normal operation carry the residue to the drain. For a condenser that looks dipped in fryer oil, we move to 5:1 and rinse with the unit pulled out and a wet vac under it.

Hard water tolerance is "Excellent," which matters in North Texas, where tap water is hard enough to leave residue with a lesser product. Safety: the concentrate is pH 12.0. Our techs wear gloves and eye protection and control the rinse. We do not hand a customer a spray bottle of this.

What you'll notice after the cleaning

The first thing a restaurant owner notices is the compressor. A grease-choked condenser cannot reject heat, so the compressor runs longer and hotter and the cabinet struggles during a dinner rush. After cleaning, the unit cycles normally and recovers faster after the door has been open.

On ice machines, a clean condenser means faster harvest cycles and fewer high-pressure shutdowns in August. On walk-ins, a clean evaporator moves the box air properly and reduces frost buildup. In kitchen HVAC, the change is airflow and smell: the dining room air stops carrying the stale-fryer note that comes from a dirty coil. We keep these claims qualitative; how much run time you save depends on how bad the coil was.

When to have this done in DFW

Kitchen grease does not follow a season, but Texas heat makes the consequences seasonal. A partly blocked condenser limps through March and fails in July.

  • Commercial kitchen refrigeration and ice machines: quarterly for every condenser within reach of the cook line. Units beside the fryer may need it every two months.
  • Walk-in coolers and freezers: evaporator cleaning twice a year, condensing unit quarterly if it shares a roof with kitchen exhaust. Schedule before Memorial Day.
  • Home kitchens with built-in refrigeration: annual condenser cleaning, ideally spring. A Sub-Zero with a clogged condenser runs hot in a closed cabinet.
  • Dust and cottonwood: outdoor condensing units near open lots or creeks collect cottonwood from late April through June. Clean before that window and check after it.

FAQ

Coil Cleaning Questions

Why do you use a fragrance-free cleaner in my restaurant?

Because the coil sits in the same airflow as your food. Blackhawk has no added fragrance, so nothing gets carried into a walk-in or across a prep line after the cleaning.

Does Blackhawk need to be rinsed?

At 20:1 the chart lists it as no rinse, which is how we apply it to indoor evaporator and reach-in coils where water cannot be used. At the stronger dilutions for heavy grease it is rinsed.

Is it safe on a Sub-Zero or True condenser?

The chart lists Blackhawk for condenser coils and as microchannel safe. We use it on built-in and commercial refrigeration condensers as part of routine maintenance.

Will cleaning the condenser fix my walk-in that will not hold temperature?

Sometimes, if a dirty coil is the cause. A coil cleaning visit is maintenance, not repair; if the unit has a failing component we will tell you, but that is a separate service.

How often should an ice machine condenser be cleaned in a kitchen?

We recommend quarterly for air-cooled machines in a commercial kitchen. Machines close to a fryer or grill may need it more often.
