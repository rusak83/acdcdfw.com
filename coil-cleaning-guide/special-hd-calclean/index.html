What Special HD CalClean is

Special HD CalClean is the heavy-duty variant of CalClean. The Nu-Calgon chart lists its special features as "Removes tobacco tar, corrosion inhibited," its soils as "Dirt/grease deposits, nicotine," and it is biodegradable, NSF Registered and microchannel safe.

It is the strongest of the heavy-duty versatile cleaners on the chart. That strength is why it lifts tar, a resinous film that normal degreasers slide over. The corrosion inhibitor lets that strength be used on aluminum and copper without eating the fins. It ships as a concentrate we dilute on site, from a very light no-rinse mix up to a strong heavy-soil mix.

What equipment we use it on (and what we don't)

On our jobs in Dallas–Fort Worth the chart's four targets look like this:

  • Electronic air cleaner cells. The collector plates in whole-house electronic air cleaners build a sticky, conductive film that shorts the cell and causes the arcing sound homeowners complain about.
  • Evaporator coils in smoking environments. Cigar lounges, bars, hookah venues, and homes or rentals where a smoker has lived for years. The indoor coil is coated with tar that has bonded with dust into a varnish.
  • Condenser coils on the same systems.
  • Commercial refrigeration in bars and restaurants. Reach-in, under-counter and walk-in cooler coils where the fouling is a mix of grease and tobacco residue. Cleaning only; we do not repair refrigeration systems on a cleaning visit.
  • Permanent filters from any of the above.

What we do not use it on: it is the wrong first choice for a residential coil with ordinary dust, where standard CalClean or Tri-Pow'r HD does the job at lower alkalinity. It is not a disinfectant, and the chart lists it for permanent filters, not air filters generally.

Why this chemistry for this job

Tar and nicotine are organic, but they are not grease. Grease lifts under mild alkalinity; tar is a resin that needs a stronger, more sustained attack. That is why Special HD CalClean runs stronger than its siblings, and why the chart calls it heavy-duty. Acidic cleaners are the wrong tool: they target mineral scale and oxidation, they are not listed as microchannel safe and they do nothing for a resin film.

Electronic air cleaner cells are a stack of thin metal plates that must come out clean and dry, with no film left to conduct between plates. The chart lists electronic cells as a target for Special HD CalClean and for no other product in the heavy-duty group.

The chart gives four working dilutions, from a very light non-rinse mix up to a heavy-soil mix. The no-rinse mix is a technician's choice for indoor coils where rinsing is impractical. In a cigar lounge or bar, the evaporator sits in a ceiling air handler above a finished room; the no-rinse mix is applied, allowed its dwell time, and the condensate carries it down the drain during normal operation. For a visibly varnished coil, we work a stronger mix and rinse. Electronic cells are removed and cleaned where they can be rinsed and dried.

Hard water tolerance is "Excellent," a practical point with hard North Texas tap water. Safety: at concentrate strength this is a caustic product. Our techs wear gloves and eye protection and control the rinse. Nothing in this article is a DIY procedure.

What you'll notice after the cleaning

The most immediate change in a smoking environment is odor. A tar-coated evaporator re-emits stale smoke into the supply air every time the blower starts; once the coil is clean, that startup smell goes with it. Landlords turning over a unit after a long-term smoker often find this is the step that finally clears it.

Airflow improves because tar binds dust into a solid mat between fins; removing it opens the passages, so registers feel stronger and the system cools faster. On bar refrigeration, a clean condenser lets the compressor cycle normally instead of running all evening. On electronic air cleaners, a clean cell stops crackling and goes back to collecting particles. We keep these claims qualitative; how much run time or energy use changes depends on how fouled the coil was.

When to have this done in DFW

  • Cigar lounges, bars and hookah venues: evaporator, condenser and bar refrigeration cleaning quarterly, with one visit in April or May before the heat.
  • Rental turnover after a smoking tenant: once, before the next tenant moves in, along with a filter change.
  • Homes with a smoker: annual evaporator cleaning in spring, and electronic air cleaner cells every three to six months depending on use.
  • Electronic air cleaners without smoking: cells still foul with cooking aerosol and dust; twice a year is typical.
  • Cottonwood and dust: the outdoor condenser follows the same DFW calendar as any other unit. Clean before the late-April to June cottonwood window and check afterward.

FAQ

Coil Cleaning Questions

Will this get the smoke smell out of my HVAC system?

If the source is tar on the evaporator coil, cleaning it with Special HD CalClean removes it. Smell that lives in ductwork, carpet or drywall is a separate problem.

Can you clean my electronic air cleaner cells with it?

Yes. The Nu-Calgon chart lists electronic cells as a target for Special HD CalClean. We remove the cells, clean them, rinse and dry them before reinstalling.

Does it need to be rinsed?

At its lightest mix the chart lists it as non-rinse, and that is what we use on indoor coils that cannot be hosed. The stronger mixes for heavy tar are rinsed.

Is it safe on a microchannel coil?

Yes. The chart lists Special HD CalClean as microchannel safe.

Do I need this if nobody smokes in my building?

Usually not. For ordinary dust and grease we use standard CalClean, Tri-Pow'r HD or Blackhawk. Special HD CalClean is the choice for tar, nicotine and electronic cells.
